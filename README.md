# Value-at-Risk (VaR) Modelling and Backtesting

## Project Overview

This project evaluates market risk for a hypothetical ₹1 crore equity portfolio comprising seven Indian paint-sector stocks. It compares three Value-at-Risk (VaR) methodologies—Parametric VaR, Historical Simulation, and Monte Carlo Simulation—and assesses their performance through backtesting and the Basel traffic-light framework.

The project demonstrates the application of quantitative finance techniques to portfolio risk measurement, model evaluation, and financial risk management using Microsoft Excel.

## Key Highlights

- **Portfolio:** ₹1 crore hypothetical portfolio of seven Indian paint-sector stocks.
- **VaR Models:** Parametric VaR, Historical Simulation, and Monte Carlo Simulation.
- **Confidence Levels:** 95% and 99%.
- **Backtesting:** Comparison of actual versus expected VaR breaches.
- **Basel Traffic-Light Test:** Classification of 99% VaR breaches over a 252-observation window.
- **Key Insight:** Correcting the daily return sign error increased Historical 99% VaR from approximately ₹3.14 lakh to ₹3.57 lakh, highlighting the importance of accurate return calculations in market risk estimation.

## Project Objectives

- Estimate one-day portfolio VaR at 95% and 99% confidence levels.
- Compare risk estimates generated by three VaR methodologies.
- Evaluate model performance using historical breach counts.
- Apply the Basel traffic-light framework to assess model outcomes.
- Identify methodological limitations and opportunities to improve risk estimation.

## Portfolio and Data

The analysis covers seven Indian paint-sector companies:

- Asian Paints
- Berger Paints
- Indigo Paints
- Kansai Nerolac
- Shalimar Paints
- Akzo Nobel India
- Sirca Paints

**Dataset details:**

- **Sample Period:** 1 April 2021 – 1 April 2026
- **Observations:** 1,240 daily closing prices and 1,239 daily returns
- **Portfolio Value:** ₹10,000,000
- **Weighting Method:** Market-capitalisation weights

The portfolio weights are based on market capitalisation and are held fixed throughout the analysis.

## Methodology

### 1. Parametric VaR

Estimates potential portfolio losses using the mean and standard deviation of portfolio returns under a normal-distribution assumption. VaR is calculated at the 95% and 99% confidence levels.

### 2. Historical Simulation VaR

Uses empirical percentiles of historical portfolio returns to estimate potential losses without imposing a normal-distribution assumption.

### 3. Monte Carlo Simulation VaR

Generates simulated portfolio returns using the estimated mean and volatility under a normal-distribution assumption. The original workbook uses 1,000 simulated draws.

### 4. Backtesting

Compares actual VaR breaches against expected breach counts to evaluate whether the estimated risk thresholds adequately capture realised losses.

### 5. Basel Traffic-Light Framework

Classifies the number of breaches at the 99% confidence level over the most recent 252 observations into Green, Yellow, or Red zones.

## Results and Key Findings

### Original Workbook Results

The initial workbook reported the following one-day VaR estimates:

| Model | 95% VaR | 99% VaR |
|---|---:|---:|
| Parametric | ₹202,307 | ₹286,822 |
| Historical Simulation | ₹198,397 | ₹314,289 |
| Monte Carlo Simulation | ₹215,593 | ₹291,931 |

### Backtesting Results

The initial workbook reported the following breach counts over 1,239 observations:

| Model | 95% Breaches | 99% Breaches |
|---|---:|---:|
| Parametric | 59 | 20 |
| Historical Simulation | 62 | 13 |
| Monte Carlo Simulation | 47 | 20 |

At the 99% confidence level, approximately 12 breaches would be expected over 1,239 observations under a correctly calibrated model.

### Independent Review and Corrected Estimates

An independent review identified a sign error in the daily return calculation. Because the price data is sorted newest-first, the original formula calculates the negative of the true return.

After correcting the return calculation:

- Historical 95% VaR increased to approximately ₹200,726.
- Historical 99% VaR increased to approximately ₹356,631.
- Parametric 99% VaR increased to approximately ₹290,179.
- The corrected historical 99% VaR was approximately 12% higher than the original estimate.

These findings demonstrate how return construction, distributional assumptions, and tail behaviour can materially affect portfolio risk estimates.

**Note:** The corrected estimates are results reported by the independent review. The complete workbook and its dashboard should be recalculated and validated before the corrected figures are treated as final model outputs.

## Limitations

- The original workbook contains a return-sign and date-alignment error.
- VaR thresholds are estimated and tested on the same historical observations, creating potential in-sample bias.
- The Monte Carlo implementation simulates portfolio returns directly rather than modelling individual assets and their correlations.
- Using only 1,000 simulations may produce unstable tail estimates.
- Portfolio risk is concentrated in Asian Paints and Berger Paints.
- Corporate actions and potentially unadjusted price observations require further verification.
- Expected Shortfall is not included in the original workbook.

## Recommended Improvements

- Correct the return calculations and regenerate all workbook outputs.
- Implement rolling-window, out-of-sample backtesting.
- Increase Monte Carlo simulations to at least 10,000 and incorporate cross-asset correlations.
- Add Expected Shortfall at 95% and 99% confidence levels.
- Conduct formal backtesting using Kupiec and Christoffersen tests.
- Evaluate alternative distributions and volatility models, including Student-t and GARCH approaches.
- Verify corporate actions and clean the underlying price data.
- Improve dashboard reporting with dynamic interpretations and risk concentration metrics.
